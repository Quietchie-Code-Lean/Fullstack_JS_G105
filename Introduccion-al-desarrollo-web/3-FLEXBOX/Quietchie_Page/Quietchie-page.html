Did you know what?

Guinea pigs, also known as cavies, are small rodents originally from the Andes in South America. Despite their name, they are neither pigs nor from Guinea. They were domesticated around 5000 B.C. by the Inca people, not as pets, but mainly for food and rituals. Today, guinea pigs are popular companions worldwide due to their gentle temperament and social nature.

A curious fact is that guinea pigs cannot produce vitamin C on their own, just like humans, so they must get it from their diet. Without enough vitamin C, they can develop scurvy. Another interesting feature is their wide range of vocalizations: they “wheek” loudly when excited, “purr” when content, and even “rumble” during mating displays. Guinea pigs also have constantly growing teeth, which is why they need to chew hay and other fibrous food to wear them down. Surprisingly, they can even recognize their owners’ voices.
